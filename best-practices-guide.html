FotoFinish Best Practices Guide

How to plan FotoFlows that cover what matters, take reference photos your team can match, and get more from every photo report.

FotoFlows

Plan FotoFlows That Cover What Matters

A FotoFlow is a virtual supervision flow for a site. Plan it around zones, not square feet.

How a FotoFlow Works

Each FotoFlow is made of slots, one for each area of the site, and each slot holds several reference photos of that area. Every shift, the app randomly picks one photo from each slot for your team to replicate.

Because your team cannot predict which view they will be asked to capture, every shift produces a daily-changing, representative report of the whole site.

Four Principles for Planning Coverage

1

Give Every Zone a Slot

List every area your team is responsible for: restrooms, entrance, lobby, kitchen, storeroom. Each one gets at least one slot. That list is your starting point.

2

Add a Slot Where the Stakes Are Higher

Client-facing areas such as entrances, lobbies, and reception, and hygiene-sensitive spaces such as restrooms, kitchens, and break rooms, justify a second slot. Ask yourself: if this area had an issue, would the client notice right away?

3

Plan Extra Coverage for Problem Areas

Zones with past complaints, high traffic, compliance requirements, or specific client expectations deserve more coverage. Documenting them protects both you and your client.

4

Let One Slot Cover a Large Area

For warehouses, hallways, and other large or uniform spaces, use one slot with several reference photos of different angles and corners. The app rotates through them over time, building a detailed record without adding work for your team.

Keep FotoFlows Working for You

Review Your Slots Every Quarter

Add zones as client needs change, and retire slots for areas that no longer need regular checks.

Go Beyond Cleaning

FotoFlows work for any visual check: health and safety postings, signage, vendor compliance, instrument readings, or franchise standards.

Reference Photos

Set the Standard with Reference Photos

Reference photos show your team what "done right" looks like. Your team frames every photo to match one, so the quality of your references shapes every report.

You set this

Reference Photo

  • Taken by a supervisor or manager when the area is freshly cleaned
  • Uploaded to a slot once and used shift after shift
  • Shows what "done correctly" looks like
  • Updated only when the standard or the space changes
Your team submits this

Evidence Photo

  • Taken by on-site staff after cleaning, in the FotoFinish app
  • A new photo every shift
  • Framed to match the reference photo shown in the app
  • Reviewed by supervisors on the dashboard

Before You Start

1

Clean the Area First

You are photographing the target condition. Never take reference photos in a dirty or partially cleaned space.

2

Choose Consistent Lighting

Turn on all artificial lights and close the blinds, or pick a time of day with stable natural light you can repeat in every season.

3

Have Your FotoFlow Open

Know which slots need reference photos before you walk the site, so you cover everything in one visit.

4

Clear the Area

Remove cleaning equipment, carts, supplies, and personal items. Only permanent fixtures should appear.

Six Rules for Every Reference Photo

1

Photograph the Standard

Shoot after a full clean. You are setting the visual target.

2

Fix the Lighting

All lights on, blinds closed, and no phone flash.

3

Include Landmarks

Frame a door, fixture, or corner your team can use to find the exact spot.

4

Show the Whole Zone

Step back so the full area is visible. Never zoom in.

5

Stay Consistent

Use the same height and distance for every slot in the FotoFlow.

6

Shoot from a Replicable Position

Hold the camera at eye level, parallel to the wall, from a spot where a team member can stand.

What to Include, Area by Area

Restrooms

  • Toilet bowl and base
  • Floor and drain visible
  • Stall door closed
  • Sink and mirror area

Lobby, Offices & Workstations

  • Entrance mat and floor
  • Full desk surface
  • Trash bin position
  • Windows and natural light

Kitchen & Break Room

  • Counter and sink basin
  • Fridge and appliance fronts
  • Floor corner showing two walls
  • Microwave or stovetop

Hallways & Common Areas

  • Left baseboard, full length
  • Right baseboard, full length
  • Wall fixtures and notice boards
  • Full length of the floor, from the center

Quick Do's and Don'ts

Do This

  • Clean the area fully before shooting
  • Turn on all lights and close the blinds
  • Keep a landmark, such as a door or fixture, in the frame
  • Frame wider so the full zone is visible
  • Take two shots per slot and upload the best one

Avoid This

  • Shooting before the clean is finished
  • Relying on natural light that shifts
  • Framing so close there is no landmark
  • Including temporary items in the frame
  • Using an angle your team cannot replicate

When to Retake

After a Renovation or Layout Change

New fixtures, furniture, or layout? Retake right away.

When Your Team Finds a Slot Hard to Match

It is usually a bad angle or a missing landmark. Review and retake it before penalizing their submissions.

Seasonally, in Spaces with Large Windows

Summer and winter light can make the same room impossible to match.

Ready to upload? Add your best shot to each slot, then save and activate the FotoFlow. See step 4 of the Setup Guide.

Your Team

Help Your Team Take Photos That Match

Brief your on-site team on what a good photo looks like for each zone. These six rules apply to every photo, every shift.

1

Light Up the Room

Turn on every light before taking the photo. Avoid the phone's flash: it causes glare on mirrors, tiles, and floors.

2

Shoot at Eye Level

Hold the phone at chest or eye level, parallel to the wall. Don't shoot from the floor or from directly above.

3

Show the Whole Area

Step back far enough to fit the entire zone in the frame. Don't zoom in.

4

Hold Steady

Use both hands and pause for a moment before tapping the shutter. Blurry photos cannot be verified.

5

Match the Reference

Look at the reference photo in the app first, then stand in the same spot and use the same angle.

6

Keep the Frame Clean

No feet, hands, mops, or carts in the shot, and no reflection in mirrors or glass.

Before Tapping "Finish"

  • Take a second shot if the first one looks blurry or dark.
  • Review each photo before confirming it: once a photo is confirmed, it cannot be retaken.
  • Take photos only after the area is fully cleaned.

Results

Get More from Your Results

Photo reports are most valuable when you act on them. Build these habits from day one.

Review the Same Day

Results are most actionable when reviewed the same day. Make a morning review a habit so issues can be addressed within 24 hours.

Act on Flagged Photos

Issue a corrective action ticket directly from any flagged photo, and use the trend charts to spot patterns across shifts or sites.

Use Photos in Client Reports

Your timestamped photo record is strong evidence of service delivery and accountability for client reporting.

Share Results with Clients

Giving clients visibility into inspection results builds trust and sets your service apart.
